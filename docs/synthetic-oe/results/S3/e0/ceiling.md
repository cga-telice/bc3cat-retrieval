## The oracle arms' advantage is exactly the 776 undecidable queries, and nothing else

Splitting each arm's identity performance at the duplicate groups makes the structure plain. One member of each group can be found and the rest cannot — **from the text**. An arm that also indexes the record's parsed `parameters` is not bound by that, because the parameters differ even where the `texto` does not.

**`bm25_unigram_params` resolves 776 of 776** — every one. `tfidf_phrases_replace` resolves 676. **No text-only arm beats chance**, and the three that sit at their corpus ceiling land on it almost exactly: 292, 293, 293 against an expectation of 292. So the two oracle arms are not *better* at identity; they are answering a different question, one in which the answer is determined. A text-only method cannot do this even in principle.

The arms *below* chance are below it for a different reason, and the column should not be read as tie-breaking skill: `bm25_unigram` at 244, `dense_e5` at 125, the GTE pair at 104 and `bge_m3_sparse` at 65 frequently retrieve no member of the group at all, so they lose these queries the same way they lose their others rather than by picking the wrong sibling.

**Headroom** is observed ÷ corpus ceiling, as D-032 requires. It exceeds 1.0 only for the two oracle arms, and that is the whole of their advantage: the ceiling binds a `texto`-only method and does not bind an arm that also indexes the record's `parameters`. On the scored column the ceiling is exactly 1.0, so there headroom is the observed value itself.

D-010 was argued from the code in S2 and measured on query sets. This is a third and sharper confirmation: **776 of 776 against a chance baseline of 292.** Neither oracle arm may be quoted without its text-only counterpart.

And on the decidable population the ranking is not what the full-population column suggests: `dense_es_hiiamsid` reads its own target back **perfectly** (1.0000), above both oracle arms. A Spanish sentence-similarity model that the previous submission never reported has the cleanest identity behaviour in the set — which says nothing yet about retrieval under variation, where it collapses to 0.0096 on `resumen`, and everything about why a ceiling must be measured per method before any degradation is read against it (D-032).

## Two arms are far below their corpus ceiling, and for different reasons

`bge_m3_sparse` at 0.0509 cannot retrieve a document from that document's own text. The gold sits at rank 2–6 with no ties, and the indexed vectors are **not** L2-normalised (norms 0.47–1.09, a 2.3× spread) against a raw dot product — so a sibling with a heavier vector out-scores the document against its own. That is BGE-M3's intended lexical scoring, so it is a ceiling rather than a defect, but it leaves the arm no headroom to lose and every later figure for it is read against 0.0509.

`bm25_unigram` at 0.8998 is below for a different reason, diagnosed in S2's `identity_misses.md`: token subsumption between maintenance bands, not duplication. The two GTE arms and `dense_e5` are below because near-identical sibling texts embed to near-identical vectors — the parametric collapse, at the encoder.
