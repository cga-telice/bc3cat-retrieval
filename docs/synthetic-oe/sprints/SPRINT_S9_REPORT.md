## Findings

Every LLM figure below (H, W, the extractor) reads the cached generation, which is the artefact: regenerating it
from scratch reproduces 98 (H) and 99 (W) of 100 queries identically and the extractor 100 of 100 (A1, A4), so a
fresh regeneration would differ on about 1–2 % of H and W queries. W figures include A3's 7 fallbacks, which keep
their text (T1 prints the count per set). Test figures are quoted from T9, which every other table now shares
(A11): one statistic, one interval.

**1. HyDE harms, or has no detectable effect, in every bin for every tested arm; no bin shows a gain** (T2, T3, T9,
Fig. 7). Y1 is supported for all five arms and Y3 for all five. Y2 is supported for none: it is **contradicted** for
`bm25_unigram` (B1 δ −0.0226 [−0.0479, −0.0101]) and ColBERT (−0.1634 [−0.2109, −0.1335]); for `dense_es_hiiamsid`
the B1 interval is negative, −0.0275 [−0.0656, −0.0043], but Holm 0.2772, so it is not supported; for
`bge_m3_dense` (−0.0069 [−0.0330, +0.0064]) and `dense_e5` (−0.0183 [−0.0344, +0.0077]) it contains 0, and so do
their B2 intervals (T2). On the identity rendering the loss is −0.3041 for BM25, −0.4296 for ColBERT and −0.8714 for
`dense_es_hiiamsid` (T1). **By the design's rule, c* is in range for all five H fits** (T3, T9): `bm25_unigram`
0.6207 [0.4686, 0.6700], ColBERT 0.3436 [−0.0400, 0.4670], `bge_m3_dense` 0.6747 [0.6340, 0.7093], `dense_e5`
0.6362 [0.5757, 0.6970], `dense_es_hiiamsid` 0.6555 [0.6241, 0.6782]. Each fit has a negative slope and so predicts
δ > 0 below c*. What was observed there (T3, post-hoc section, A11; descriptive) does not show it: `bm25_unigram`
−0.0240 [−0.0628, −0.0086] and `dense_es_hiiamsid` −0.0317 [−0.0791, −0.0068] are negative; `bge_m3_dense` −0.0107
[−0.0372, +0.0024] and `dense_e5` −0.0179 [−0.0351, +0.0090] contain 0; ColBERT has 1 scored query below its c*
(‡). The region is mostly `stacked_texto` (678 of BM25's 748 scored queries there), whose untransformed item
accuracy is near the floor (0.0516 for BM25), and without it the cells are still negative or contain 0. So c* is
reported as the rule defines it, a property of a straight-line fit to these renderings, and not as a region where
expansion was observed to pay.

**2. Rewriting harms the dense arms near-verbatim and helps BM25** (T1, T2, T3, T9). Y1 is supported for ColBERT
(B4 −0.0924), `bge_m3_dense` (−0.1311) and `dense_es_hiiamsid` (−0.2073); for `dense_e5` the B4 δ is −0.0209
[−0.0459, −0.0021], Holm 0.3744, not supported. For `bm25_unigram` the B4 δ is positive, +0.1135 [+0.0413,
+0.1480] (Holm 0.3172, so not contradicted), and on `single_texto` W lifts it +0.0997 [+0.0460, +0.1217] (T1). Its
Y3 slope is positive, +0.2376 [+0.0930, +0.3122], against the prediction, so its fit has no crossing in range. Y2
is supported for no arm and contradicted for none: every B1 interval contains 0. The nearest to a gain is
`dense_es_hiiamsid`, +0.0189 [−0.0060, +0.0436], query-level [+0.0057, +0.0321]. By the rule, c* is in range for the
other four W fits: ColBERT 0.6691 [0.6134, 0.7017], `bge_m3_dense` 0.6888 [0.6499, 0.7217], `dense_e5` 0.6071
[−0.3855, 0.7307], `dense_es_hiiamsid` 0.6964 [0.6562, 0.7189]. Below them (T3, A11) the observed δ has a
concept-level interval containing 0 for every arm; `dense_es_hiiamsid`'s +0.0175 [−0.0073, +0.0414] is positive only
at query level, [+0.0047, +0.0303], and turns to −0.0054 [−0.0275, +0.0095] without `stacked_texto`. No paired W − H
contrast was run, so the two transforms are not ranked against each other here.

**3. The canonicaliser recovers most of BM25's number-word damage and none of the synonym damage** (T4, T9).
Z1 is supported for `bm25_unigram` +0.2671 [+0.1438, +0.3445], `dense_es_hiiamsid` +0.2469 [+0.1609, +0.2968] and
ColBERT +0.0309 [+0.0157, +0.0507], the last not robust to D-004 (T8). It is not supported for `dense_e5`, +0.0318
[+0.0148, +0.0431] (Holm 0.0576), nor for `bge_m3_dense`, +0.0251 [−0.0027, +0.0651] (Holm 0.9739). By type for BM25
(T4), the cells with 10 or more concepts: `unit_conversion` +0.1849 [+0.0050, +0.5139] on 12 concepts,
`unit_expansion` +0.0262 [+0.0029, +0.0642] on 16, `synonym_label` +0.0000 on 37, as S6 predicted. The largest cell,
`num_to_text` +0.7475 [+0.6654, +0.8148], is on **9 concepts (‡)**: printed, not read. The L1 row, which pools them,
is Z1. **Z2 holds by construction**: C changes no corpus text (`tests/test_canon.py`), so every `texto_u` and
`resumen_u` query is unchanged and δ is +0.0000. Z2 is reported as supported and tests nothing.

**4. Why TF-IDF escapes the rare-code pattern, and the IDF cap** (T5). On BM25's parent misses on coded `resumen`,
rare tokens carry a median 0.6605 of the rank-1 score (above half in 83.4 %); on TF-IDF's, 0.0000. The median IDF
of rare against other query tokens is 7.82 times for BM25 and 2.42 for TF-IDF, and BM25 multiplies the query's IDF
by a document weight that already carries it. Capping IDF at df 1,200 (4.0692) reaches 468 of 682 BM25 terms, not
only the codes. G1 is supported, parent +0.0661 [+0.0225, +0.1299] on `resumen_u`; G2 too, and not as
non-inferiority alone: the cap **improves** `single_texto` by +0.0966 [+0.0652, +0.1276]. On the four bases with 10
or more concepts no capped δ is negative, for either arm, at either level, and most are positive, including identity
(`bm25_unigram` 0.8934 → 0.9082) and stacked parent level (+0.2150). `single_l2_texto` (9 concepts, ‡) points the
same way and is not read. The cut was read on dev `resumen` (S91), so every guard figure is in-sample.

**5. The published LLM extractor does not beat the rules pipeline; a schema defect, not value reading, is why**
(T6). Every figure here carries the extractor's exposure: its prompts were chosen on OEB `resumen`, whose concepts
sit on both sides of OE's split. E1 and E2 are not supported: on pooled L1 the arm reaches 0.2719 against 0.2478
(`rules_valuenorm`) and 0.2551 (BM25), concept intervals [−0.0881, +0.2507] and [−0.0688, +0.1812]; over all single
types it falls to 0.2068. Post hoc (A7, A8): the arm loses 16,401 of 51,838 axes (31.6 %), 15,046 of them because
`OE_concept_schema.json` names axes with stray spaces that the model writes trimmed and the source reads exactly.
The key-tolerant variant, same cached responses, reaches 0.7830 on L1 (+0.5279 [+0.4370, +0.5800] over BM25,
+0.5439 [+0.4904, +0.6027] over S5's **literal-oracle bound**, which reads schema literals and leaves a rewritten
axis blank, so it bounds a literal-reading extractor, not this one), 0.8157 over all single types and 0.6780 on
stacked. That is descriptive, unregistered, exposed as above, and in-sample in the sense that the cause was found
after the result.

**6. Cost** (T7). Generation, median seconds per query: HyDE 1.24–1.73, rewrite 1.15–4.07, extraction 1.03 per call.
Retrieval stays in milliseconds per query except ColBERT, 0.4361 s under H (0.2634 in the earlier untransformed runs, a different query mix). 7 rewrite fallbacks, all A3
truncations; 13 HyDE outputs carry non-Latin script, 11 of them on `resumen_u`.

## What is now known to be wrong

1. **H6's premise that expansion pays below an overlap threshold, as a finding about these renderings.** On OE
   dev, with these renderings and this model, no bin shows a gain under HyDE or rewriting, and HyDE harms the two arms
   for which Y2 is contradicted. The design's rule yields c* for 9 of 10 fits, and those values are reported (findings
   1–2, T3); what is wrong is reading c* as the edge of a region where expansion helps, because the observed δ below
   it is negative or indistinguishable from zero (T3, A11), and coverage there is confounded with `stacked_texto`.
2. **The published extractor as a test of LLM value reading.** It is defeated by `OE_concept_schema.json`'s padded
   axis names before any value is read. Recorded as `DATASET_DEFECTS.md` P9; the harness should strip axis labels
   wherever it compares them (D-056).
3. **`bm25_unigram`'s identity ceiling as the method's.** Capping IDF raises identity item Acc@1 from 0.8934 to
   0.9082: part of what S3 read as BM25's ceiling is its unbounded IDF.
4. **Z2 as a test.** C is the identity on every corpus text, so Z2 could not fail; registering it added a supported
   reading that carries no information. The same holds for C's 14 runs on `texto_u` and `resumen_u`.
5. **The design's expectation that the guard would trade identity accuracy for robustness** (G2 written as
   non-inferiority). It costs nothing anywhere on dev.
