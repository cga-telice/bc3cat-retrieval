## Findings

**1. HyDE hurts every tested arm at every overlap; there is no crossover** (T2, T3, T9, Fig. 7). Y1 is supported
for all five arms, Y3 for all five, Y2 for none, and contradicted for `bm25_unigram` (B1 δ −0.0226 [−0.0487,
−0.0105]) and ColBERT (−0.1634 [−0.2132, −0.1347]). On the identity rendering the loss is −0.3041 for BM25,
−0.4296 for ColBERT and −0.8714 for `dense_es_hiiamsid` (T1). The design's decision rule prints a c*, the coverage
where the fitted line crosses zero (`bm25_unigram` 0.6207, `bge_m3_dense` 0.6747, ColBERT 0.3436), but the lowest
bin's observed δ is negative or indistinguishable from zero for every arm, so c* is where a straight line
extrapolates to zero, not a region where expansion pays. It is not a threshold a practitioner can use.

**2. Rewriting costs less than HyDE and helps BM25 near-verbatim** (T1, T2, T9). Y1 is supported for ColBERT
(B4 −0.0924), `bge_m3_dense` (−0.1311) and `dense_es_hiiamsid` (−0.2073), not for `dense_e5`. For `bm25_unigram`
the B4 δ is positive, +0.1135 [+0.0429, +0.1474] (Holm 0.3172, so not contradicted), and on `single_texto` W lifts
it +0.0997 [+0.0460, +0.1217]. Its Y3 slope is positive, +0.2376, against the prediction. Y2 is supported for no arm.

**3. The canonicaliser recovers most of BM25's number-word damage and none of the synonym damage** (T4, T9).
Z1 is supported for `bm25_unigram` +0.2671 [+0.1438, +0.3445], `dense_es_hiiamsid` +0.2469 and ColBERT
+0.0309, the last not robust to D-004 (T8); not supported for `bge_m3_dense` and `dense_e5` (+0.0318, Holm 0.0576).
By type for BM25: `num_to_text` +0.7475 [+0.6654, +0.8148], `unit_conversion` +0.1849 [+0.0050, +0.5139],
`unit_expansion` +0.0262, `synonym_label` +0.0000, as S6 predicted. **Z2 holds by construction**: C changes no corpus
text (`tests/test_canon.py`), so every `texto_u` and `resumen_u` query is unchanged and δ is
+0.0000. Z2 is reported as supported and tests nothing.

**4. Why TF-IDF escapes the rare-code pattern, and the IDF cap** (T5). On BM25's parent misses on coded `resumen`,
rare tokens carry a median 0.6605 of the rank-1 score (above half in 83.4 %); on TF-IDF's, 0.0000. The median IDF
of rare against other query tokens is 7.82 times for BM25 and 2.42 for TF-IDF, and BM25 multiplies the query's IDF
by a document weight that already carries it. Capping IDF at df 1,200 (4.0692) reaches 468 of 682 BM25 terms, not
only the codes. G1 is supported, parent +0.0661 [+0.0225, +0.1299] on `resumen_u`; G2 too, and not as
non-inferiority alone: the cap **improves** `single_texto` by +0.0966 [+0.0652, +0.1276]. It lowers no base for either
arm and raises most, including identity (`bm25_unigram` 0.8934 → 0.9082) and stacked parent level (+0.2150). The cut was
read on dev `resumen` (S91), so every guard figure is in-sample.

**5. The published LLM extractor does not beat the rules pipeline; a schema defect, not value reading, is why**
(T6). E1 and E2 are not supported: on pooled L1 the arm reaches 0.2719 against 0.2478 (`rules_valuenorm`) and
0.2551 (BM25), concept intervals [−0.0881, +0.2507] and [−0.0688, +0.1812]; over all single types it falls to
0.2068. Post hoc (A7, A8): the arm loses 16,401 of 51,838 axes (31.6 %), 15,046 of them because
`OE_concept_schema.json` names axes with stray spaces that the model writes trimmed and the source reads exactly.
The key-tolerant variant, same cached responses, reaches 0.7830 on L1 (+0.5279 [+0.4370, +0.5800] over BM25,
+0.5439 over the oracle bound), 0.8157 over all single types and 0.6780 on stacked. That is descriptive, unregistered
and in-sample in the sense that the cause was found after the result.

**6. Cost** (T7). Generation, median seconds per query: HyDE 1.24–1.73, rewrite 1.15–4.07, extraction 1.03 per call.
Retrieval stays in milliseconds per query except ColBERT, 0.4361 s under H (0.2634 in the earlier untransformed runs, a different query mix). 7 rewrite fallbacks, all A3
truncations; 13 HyDE outputs carry non-Latin script, 11 of them on `resumen_u`.

## What is now known to be wrong

1. **H6's premise that expansion pays below an overlap threshold.** On OE dev, with these renderings and this
   model, HyDE is harmful at every overlap and rewriting is at best neutral at low overlap. The design's decision
   rule (c* from a linear fit) yields a number with no support in the bins and must not be reported as a threshold.
2. **The published extractor as a test of LLM value reading.** It is defeated by `OE_concept_schema.json`'s padded
   axis names before any value is read. Recorded as `DATASET_DEFECTS.md` P9; the harness should strip axis labels
   wherever it compares them (D-056).
3. **`bm25_unigram`'s identity ceiling as the method's.** Capping IDF raises identity item Acc@1 from 0.8934 to
   0.9082: part of what S3 read as BM25's ceiling is its unbounded IDF.
4. **Z2 as a test.** C is the identity on every corpus text, so Z2 could not fail; registering it added a supported
   reading that carries no information. The same holds for C's 14 runs on `texto_u` and `resumen_u`.
5. **The design's expectation that the guard would trade identity accuracy for robustness** (G2 written as
   non-inferiority). It costs nothing anywhere on dev.
