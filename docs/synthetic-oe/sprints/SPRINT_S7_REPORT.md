## Findings

**1. The headline: under stacking no arm keeps half its item-level accuracy** (T1, tie-free). ColBERT has the highest
deployable point estimate, 0.4128 (identity 0.9993; δ −0.5865 [−0.6566, −0.4145] by concept); the oracle RRF prints
0.4108. No paired test between arms was run, so neither ordering is a tested claim. Tuned BM25 falls from 0.9069 to 0.1061 (δ −0.8008 [−0.8685, −0.6311]); its oracle twin from 0.9992 to
0.1772; `bge_m3_dense` to 0.1679; `dense_es_hiiamsid`, perfect at identity on these leaves (1.0000), to 0.1135.
`dense_e5` loses least in absolute terms (0.5081 → 0.1894) because it starts lowest; retention, not δ, sets arms
side by side (0.3456 for E5 against 0.4130 for ColBERT and 0.1113 for BM25). The published rules pipeline ends at
0.1298 (`rules_valuenorm`), and its oracle-extraction bound at 0.1756. Three floor arms (`bge_m3_sparse`, both GTE)
are printed and not read.

**2. Stacking is far worse than any single edit, for every arm** (T2, between-population, not a contrast). BM25's
δ is −0.8008 stacked, −0.4122 pooled over the nine single types (S4's population) and −0.1081 on L2w; ColBERT's
−0.5865, −0.2136 and −0.1485. The sets reach different leaves, so this is description, not a dose effect.

**3. For the encoders the collapse is the predicted one: the concept holds, the leaf goes** (T1 parent level; W1,
W2). ColBERT keeps parent level at 0.9837, E5 at 0.9845, the oracle TF-IDF at 0.9638 and the structured arms at
0.9722 while item level falls. W1 (the gap widens) is supported for all seven tested arms and W2 (discrimination
falls more than concept identification) for the five non-BM25 arms, tie-free, robust to D-004 and as run.

**4. For BM25 it is not: under stacking BM25 loses the concept** (T1, T3). `bm25_unigram`'s parent level falls
from 0.9980 to 0.2931, its oracle twin's from 1.0000 to 0.3967; on OEB, where 1,794 of the 2,521 queries sit, to
0.1594. W2 is therefore not supported for either BM25 arm, and its estimate has the wrong sign: −0.1925
[−0.2786, −0.0255] for `bm25_unigram` (Holm p 0.1470, so *not supported* rather than *contradicted* under S4's
rule) and −0.0800 [−0.1635, +0.1473] for the oracle. The gap still widens (W1 supported, +0.0810 and +0.2052),
but because item level falls further, not because parent level holds. S6 found the lexical arm keeping the concept
under single edits; with every applicable edit stacked, it does not.

**5. The sibling-density law is not established** (W3; T4). Larger families lose more in every arm's point
estimate, and all seven concept intervals exclude 0. Only `dense_es_hiiamsid` is supported (+0.0360
[+0.0229, +0.0546], Holm p 0.0042). The other six fail Holm, `bm25_unigram` narrowly (+0.0990 [+0.0711, +0.1241],
raw p 0.0064, Holm p 0.0512). Their raw p-values agree with their intervals; the Holm correction across the 21 tests
is what separates interval and reading (corrected after the audit, A6 c).

The design fixed a within-OEB slope in advance as the subchapter check (T4, descriptive). Inside OEB (13 concepts,
1,794 queries scored) larger families still lose more, with a concept interval excluding 0 for five of seven arms.
Examples are `bm25_unigram` +0.1179 [+0.0401, +0.1752], `bge_m3_colbert` +0.0496 [+0.0280, +0.0700] and
`dense_es_hiiamsid` +0.0273 [+0.0030, +0.0539]. The exceptions are `tfidf_phrases_replace` and `dense_e5`. So the
association is not only a difference between OEB and the rest. It is, however, not separated from the dose: log₂
family size correlates 0.5984 with it, and the within-OEB slope does not control it.

The slope outside OEB (A4, post hoc) is uninformative rather than absent. Its 26 concepts (672 queries scored) hold
no family above 2,160 leaves, and its concept intervals contain 0 for six of seven arms, for example
+0.0393 [−0.0459, +0.0771] for BM25. The exception is the oracle twin, −0.0597 [−0.1412, −0.0206]. For
`dense_es_hiiamsid` the point estimate outside OEB (+0.0475) is larger than the full-population slope.
Leave-one-out moves no slope much (`bm25_unigram` +0.0927 to +0.1013), so no single concept carries it.

**6. `bm25_unigram`'s W3 reading is not robust.** Adjusted for dose it reads supported (+0.0763 [+0.0460, +0.0999]);
unadjusted it does not. The tie-free unadjusted reading stands (A1 d). The adjustment cannot address type mix
(A2): every item-scored query carries an L1 edit.

**7. Dose and type presence, descriptive only** (T5, T6). BM25's δ deepens with the visible dose, from −0.5606 at
2 to −1.0000 at 6, but the concepts behind each dose fall 27 → 17 → 8 → 2 → 1, so each step changes family as much
as dose. `synonym_label` is in 2,411 of the 2,466 item-scored queries; the 55 without it show a BM25 δ of +0.0000,
too few and too concentrated to read. **E2 cannot identify interactions**: `reorder` is in no dev stacked query,
`template_paraphrase` in every one. No interaction claim and no H4 claim follows from S7.

**8. The re-runs reproduce S2 exactly** ([`s2_regression.md`](../results/S7/s2_regression.md)). All five arms
agree with S2's archived runs on rank 1 for 2,521 of 2,521 queries, including the rules arms whose retrieval path
changed in S5: on stacked, that change moves no rank 1.

## What is now known to be wrong

1. **"Parent level stays near its ceiling across all conditions"** (H1's first clause) is wrong for BM25 under
   stacking: 0.2931 tie-free from 0.9980. BM25 does not show parametric collapse on stacked queries. It shows
   plain retrieval failure: it loses the family, not just the sibling. This is BM25's, not the lexical family's:
   the oracle TF-IDF keeps parent level at 0.9638.
2. **"Larger families lose more"** is not established at the registered threshold, and not shown wrong. It
   holds descriptively within OEB for five of seven arms, but it is not separated from the dose, and the set
   outside OEB is too small-familied to test it.
3. **The design's L1-present covariate** carried no information: every item-scored query carries an L1 edit (A2).
   The design did not check it.
4. **T1's "right parent, wrong item" column equals Δ stacked by construction**, since an item hit implies a
   parent hit. It is printed but adds nothing; read Δ.
5. **The pantry artefacts are common on stacked.** 720 of 2,521 dev queries are flagged (T7), in 25 concepts. No
   reading changes without them, but "documented stress" understates their reach on this set.

## What the next sprint inherits

- **S8**: H4 on the nested ladder, blind to everything here except T5, which reads between concepts. The dose and
  the family confound each other on stacked (finding 7); the ladder removes that by construction.
- **S10/S11**: under stacking ColBERT's losses stay within the concept (parent 0.9837), BM25's do not. Any
  within-family reranker built on a BM25 first stage inherits a first stage that has lost the family.
- **S12**: re-read W1–W3 on test stacked under A1–A2, and print the within-OEB and outside-OEB slopes beside W3,
  both descriptive (D-051 amendment). **S8** is where family size and the dose can be separated.

  the BGE-M3 server's reachability from `bc3cat-s3` depends on Docker Desktop's `host.docker.internal` (A3).
