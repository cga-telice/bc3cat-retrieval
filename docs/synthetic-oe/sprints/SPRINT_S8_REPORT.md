## Findings

**1. Accuracy falls with every edit added to a leaf, for every arm** (T1, T6; X3 supported for all seven). Item
Acc@1 at rung 5 is 0.0009 for `bm25_unigram` (identity 0.9405), 0.0041 for its oracle twin, 0.2923 for ColBERT
and 0.0488 for `dense_es_hiiamsid` (both 1.0000 at identity). The within-leaf slopes run from −0.0551 (`dense_e5`,
which starts at 0.3872) to −0.1918. Every arm's quadratic term is positive, so the loss decelerates as leaves run
out of accuracy to lose. No arm shows the accelerating curve that super-additivity predicts.

**2. Cumulatively, the stack does about what the clipped sum of its single edits predicts** (T4, Fig. 6; X1). The
observed curve tracks the prediction closely for six of seven arms. X1 is supported only for `dense_e5`,
−0.0671 [−0.1029, −0.0328], negative in all four concepts. That reading is **not robust**: without the
pantry-artefact queries it is −0.0524 and not supported (T9). For ColBERT the stack does slightly *better* than
predicted, +0.0199 [−0.0234, +0.0643], as does `bge_m3_dense`, +0.0343 [+0.0000, +0.0694]. Neither excludes 0.

**3. X1 cannot see super-additivity for BM25** (T4). At rungs 2–5, 0.8018 to 0.9817 of `bm25_unigram`'s leaves
are already predicted to fail. One L1 edit alone breaks most leaves: `unit_expansion` alone gives δ −0.9192 and
`num_to_text` −0.8716 (T2). With the prediction at 0, a leaf can only count as sub-additive. The design named this
risk. BM25's X1 of −0.0015 is no evidence either way.

**4. An added edit breaks a surviving leaf more often in company than alone** (T5; X2 supported for six of seven
arms, not `dense_e5`). ColBERT +0.1271 [+0.0916, +0.1632]; `tfidf_phrases_replace` +0.1010 [+0.0803, +0.1238];
`dense_es_hiiamsid` +0.1080. Two readings are marginal: `bm25_unigram` (+0.0643) and `bge_m3_dense` (+0.0738)
each have Holm p 0.0486. Both read *not supported* as run and on the clean subset (T9). **Only one excess clears
the draw-noise bar the design set** (finding 5): `tfidf_phrases_replace`, against a noise rate of 0.0863. ColBERT's
+0.1271 sits below its 0.2171, and so do the other four. Under the design's own rule, five of the six supported X2
readings cannot be attributed to interaction.

**5. The noise bar is inflated for the lexical arms, so it is not a clean measure** (T3; see known-wrong 2).
T3's disagreement counts an expected tie-break as a disagreement. Where two identical queries tie at rank 1, they
disagree in expectation although they are the same query. That is why `tfidf_phrases_replace` shows more
disagreement on text-equal leaves (0.1489) than on text-different ones (0.0863). For `bge_m3_dense`, `dense_e5`
and `dense_es_hiiamsid` the text-equal rate is 0.0000, so their bar is draw noise alone. ColBERT's text-equal rate is
0.0593 against 0.2171 text-different, so ties are a small part of its bar. For the dense encoders the bar stands; for
BM25 and TF-IDF it overstates the noise, and ColBERT's is slightly inflated.

**6. G3: H4 is identifiable within leaf for 34 of 36 type pairs on 328 dev leaves; across concepts it is not
(4 clusters).** `reorder` × `template_paraphrase` and `unit_conversion` × `unit_expansion` never co-occur (T7).
The design answers the gate in these words; the result does not change them.

**7. On the ladder, the BM25 arms lose the concept and the encoders keep it** (T1 parent level, descriptive). At
rung 5, BM25's parent Acc@1 is 0.0884 and its oracle twin's 0.1250, against 0.9970 for ColBERT. `dense_e5` and the
oracle TF-IDF stay at 1.0000. S7's D-050 pattern recurs here with family held fixed within each leaf.

**8. The stacked set's dose gradient was not just family** (T8, between-population, descriptive). At dose 2, S7's
stacked BM25 δ is −0.5606 over 27 concepts; the ladder's is −0.7510 on four. At dose 3 ColBERT is −0.6000 stacked
and −0.5174 on the ladder. The two gradients are of the same order, one between families and one within leaf.
Nothing here is a contrast.

## What is now known to be wrong

1. **The design's H4 rule labels "no arm holds both tests" as *partly supported*.** The rule has three outcomes and
   assumes at least one arm would hold both. The literal label overstates the evidence; D-054 asks César to restate
   H4.
2. **A2 (d)'s noise bar mixes tie-break noise with draw noise** for arms with rank-1 ties (finding 5). It is
   conservative for BM25 and TF-IDF. A tie-free bar that excludes ties within one query was not specified, and is
   not computed here.
3. **"Stacked edits jointly exhaust redundancy" (H4's mechanism)** predicts an accelerating loss. Every arm's
   curve decelerates (finding 1), and cumulatively the stack is close to the clipped sum (finding 2).
4. **The plan's "additive prediction from S4's isolated effects"** would have been wrong by a wide margin. BM25's
   `unit_expansion` δ alone is −0.9192 on the ladder leaves against −0.4153 on S4's population (T2). The E3
   isolated set on the same leaves was the right reference (D-052).
5. **Pantry artefacts are common on the ladder**: 481 of 1,640 rung queries and 316 of 2,952 isolated queries are
   flagged (T9). Three readings change without them, all toward *not supported*.
