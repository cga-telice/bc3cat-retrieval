## Findings

Every figure below is printed, with its n and its sources, in the generated table named beside it.

**1. The identity ceiling separates oracle arms from deployable ones.** For a `texto`-only method the
corpus permits at most **0.9863** — 776 leaves in 292 groups share a `texto`, so one member of each is
findable and 484 are not ([`ceiling.md`](../results/S3/e0/ceiling.md)). `bm25_unigram_params`
resolves **776 of 776** undecidable queries and `tfidf_phrases_replace` 676. Their own indexed fields
have their own ceilings: `text_word_params` has no duplicates (1.0000), and
`text_word_phrases_replace` has 100 unavoidable misses (0.9972) — exactly the 100 that
`tfidf_phrases_replace` fails to resolve. The three text-only arms at their ceiling land on 292, 293
and 293 against a chance expectation of 292. The two extra hits are **tie-breaking, not resolution**:
the gold's score equals rank 1's on 757 (`bge_m3_dense`) and 770 (`dense_es_hiiamsid`) of the 776, and
neither top-k selection — FAISS `index.search` for `bge_m3_dense`, `np.argpartition` for
`dense_es_hiiamsid` — orders an exact tie by anything the query controls, so queries with the same text
can resolve the same tie differently. On the rest (19 and 6), a sibling with the *same indexed text*
outscores the gold: identical inputs did not always encode to identical scores. Non-reproducible
batched GPU encoding would do this; it was not tested. The oracle arms are not better at identity; they are answering a question whose
answer the parameters determine. D-010 was argued from the code in S2 and measured on query sets;
this is a third confirmation.

**2. On the decidable population, `dense_es_hiiamsid` is perfect (1.0000)** — above both oracle arms —
and falls to **0.0093** on `resumen` (n = 34,646 scored). The arm the previous submission never
reported has the cleanest identity behaviour in the set. That is D-032's argument in one row: a
ceiling is a per-method property, and no degradation means anything until it is read against one.

**3. The replication does not reproduce the previous headline on OE.** Tuned BM25 reaches **0.6394**
on OE dev `resumen→texto` against 0.974 on OEB test; `bm25_unigram` reaches **0.0142** against 0.869
(0.0148 at the previous study's own `k1`=0.80). All three are item-level on the duplicate-free
population, n = 34,646. The two columns are reference, not a contrast, and S3 does **not** establish
why they differ. Two measurements bear on it, and neither is causal:
- **Tuning is untested on `resumen`.** The sweep ran on `texto`, `single_texto` and `stacked_texto`,
  where `k1`=0.60/`b`=0.35 is the unique argmax in 3 of 6 cells, ties exactly in a fourth, and is
  beaten in the other two by nothing detectable
  (+0.0014, p = 0.8507; +0.0049, p = 0.4250). On `resumen` there are only the two points above.
- **OE's summaries are less verbatim than OEB's.** `resumen→texto` lexical coverage is **79.38 %** on
  OE dev against **94.10 %** on OEB ([`overlap.md`](../results/S3/overlap/overlap.md)). That compares
  two populations that also differ in chapter, corpus and split (and 22,305 of the OEB pairs are on
  OE's test side). It is a description consistent with the gap, not a measurement of its cause. The
  design keeps overlap descriptive in S3, and a within-OE test belongs to S6.

**Chapter is confounded with regime**, so the claim is *"does not replicate on OE"*, not "the number
was wrong".

**4. Numeric coverage is blind to two of the four L1 types, and this survives the D-004
sensitivity check.** `num_to_text` removes exactly one number per query (4.14 → 3.14 against the same
gold) and `unit_expansion` 0.64, by spelling them out. The number is not *missed*; it is gone from the
query, so coverage stays pinned at 100 %. Only `unit_conversion`, which rewrites a value, shows as
lost coverage (79.14 %, with 57.35 % of its queries keeping all their numbers — point estimates;
`overlap.md` carries no interval on numeric coverage). Doubling is heaviest in `num_to_text` (30.5 %),
then `unit_expansion` (17.2 %) and `synonym_label` (16.2 %). With both pantry artefacts excluded, the
pattern holds at −1.00, −0.52 and 79.14 % respectively. H5 must be stated over coverage **and** the
query-vs-gold number count. That re-specification rests on the two Δ numbers, whose concept-clustered
intervals exclude 0 ([−1.00, −1.00] and [−1.03, −0.15] clean); `unit_conversion`'s coverage figure
illustrates it but carries no interval, and its Δ numbers interval [−0.54, +0.19] straddles 0.

**5. S2's by-dose strata were misattributed, not merely uncertain.** Of the 730 dev queries S2 called
dose 5, **193** show five modifications in the TEXTO. The corrected table also exposes a confound the
old one hid: concepts per cell fall 27 → 17 → 8 → 2 → 1 as dose rises, so a slope read off these rows
confounds dose with family — which is why H4 is answered on E3 in S8 and not here. The top dose is a
single concept, and the overlap table prints no interval for it rather than one of width zero.

## What is now known to be wrong

1. **"`b` dominates and more length normalisation monotonically hurts"** — the first close's reading
   of the sweep surface. Accuracy falls at every step up in `b` in only 5 of 30 rows; the surface is
   single-peaked in 28, with the peak at `b` = 0.35 in 21. And the OEB queries the point was fitted to
   average **20.8** normalised tokens, not "~14".
2. **"`bge_m3_sparse` may survive L1 variation because learned sparse expands tokens."** My own
   framing in D-012, and wrong. Its identity ceiling is **0.0501** scored (0.0509 over all 35,422): it
   cannot retrieve a document from that document's own text. Its vectors are not L2-normalised (norms
   0.47–1.09) and are scored by a raw dot product. The first close also said the gold sat "at rank
   2–6, no ties". It does not: of 32,909 misses the gold is at rank 2–100 (median 18) for most, past
   rank 100 for 12,192, and tied with rank 1 for 8. A ceiling, not a defect — and the arm has no
   headroom left to lose.
3. **"The identity shortfall of `bge_m3_colbert` was a method property."** 484 of its 492 misses were
   undecidable duplicates; 8 remain of 34,646. G1 called this arm *ambiguous* on the strength of a
   corpus defect. **G1 is not re-read** (D-032), but the reading is now known to have been about the
   corpus.
4. **"The harness runs the method set."** It ran the five arms S2 needed. Widening to ten broke it
   twice: five retrievers scored every query in one block and lost **12 of 17 runs** to
   `DeadKernelError` (H6), and `bge_m3_dense`'s builder wrote its FAISS index outside
   `index/{collection}/` so both its runs failed *after* the build reported success, 70,242 docs and a
   clean stamp (H7). `num_docs` is not an integrity check.
5. **"A clean tree stamps clean."** A host/container line-ending disagreement stamped all seven
   indexes `code_dirty: true` over a file nobody had touched (H5). The same phantom explains the two
   dirty paths on the S1 `resumen` run archived under A2 — that archiving was right, but for a
   different reason than stated. The runner-side check that followed lives in the git-ignored
   `logs/S3/*.sh`, not in committed code; the stamps are the guarantee.
6. **"`requirements.txt`'s pin runs the method set."** `Alibaba-NLP/gte-multilingual-base` cannot be
   loaded under it by either path (D-034).
7. **"The manuscript's GTE rows identify their model."** Config, prose and results table name three
   different things; those rows are not reproducible from `(config, code commit)` (D-035). The third
   reporting defect of that submission, after the unreported `hiiamsid` row and the wrong README table.
8. **"`index/*/meta.json` stamps what produced an index."** It records `{python, sklearn}` and not the
   ML stack, so for a neural arm operating rule 1's provenance set is incomplete (H4).
9. **"A generator that computes its tables computes its prose."** `build_results_e0.py` computed its
   tables and typed its prose, and the first close reported two p-values (0.845 / 0.417) that no
   committed code produced, beside a table printing 0.8507 / 0.4250. Found by the audit, not by me.
10. **"No number in the prose is typed"** — the first remediation's claim. Its guard covered two of
    the three generators; `build_results_s3.py` still typed eight numbers and kept a withdrawn
    sentence. Found by the re-audit.
11. **"4 of 6 cells have the transferred point as argmax"**, and a ceiling column that gave the oracle
    arms the `texto` ceiling. One "win" was an exact tie broken by iteration order; the oracle arms'
    fields have their own ceilings (finding 1). Both found by the re-audit.
