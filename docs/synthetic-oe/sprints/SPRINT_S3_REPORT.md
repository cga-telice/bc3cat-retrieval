## Findings

**1. The identity ceiling is an instrument, and it separates oracle from deployable categorically.**
For a `texto`-only method the corpus permits at most **0.9863** — 776 leaves in 292 groups share a
`texto`, so one member of each is findable and 484 are not. Splitting each arm at those queries
([`ceiling.md`](../results/S3/e0/ceiling.md)): `bm25_unigram_params` resolves **776 of 776**,
`tfidf_phrases_replace` 676, and **no text-only arm beats the chance baseline of 292** — the three at
their ceiling land on 292, 293, 293. Headroom (observed ÷ ceiling) exceeds 1.0 only for the two
oracle arms: 1.013 and 1.011, against ColBERT's 1.000. The oracle arms are not better at identity;
they are answering a question whose answer is determined for them. D-010 was argued from the code in
S2 and measured on query sets; this is a third confirmation and the only categorical one.

**2. On the decidable population, `dense_es_hiiamsid` is perfect (1.0000)** — above both oracle arms —
and collapses to **0.0093** on `resumen` (n=34,646 scored). The arm the previous submission never
reported has the cleanest
identity behaviour in the set. That is D-032's argument in one row: a ceiling is a per-method
property, and no degradation means anything until it is read against one.

**3. The replication does not reproduce the previous headline, and the mechanism is measured.** Tuned
BM25 reaches **0.6394** on OE dev `resumen→texto` against 0.974 on OEB test; `bm25_unigram` reaches
**0.0142** against 0.869 (0.0148 at the previous study's own `k1`=0.80, so not an artefact of the
point carried). All three are item-level on the duplicate-free population, n=34,646. Two findings
settle why:
- **the operating point transfers** — in 4 of 6 sweep cells the argmax *is* `k1`=0.60/`b`=0.35, and
  the two exceptions are noise (+0.0014 p=0.845, +0.0049 p=0.417, near-symmetric flip counts);
- **OE is 15 points less verbatim than OEB** — `resumen→texto` lexical coverage **79.38 %** against
  OEB's 94.09 %, measured with the definition that reproduces the review analysis's own figures to
  0.01 pp with the median exact.

The previous headline was measured on the chapter where the task is most nearly lexical containment.
**Chapter is confounded with regime**, so the claim is *"does not replicate on OE"*, not "the number
was wrong".

**4. Numeric coverage is blind to two of the four L1 types.** `num_to_text` removes exactly one number
per query (4.14 → 3.14 against the same gold) and `unit_expansion` 0.64, by spelling them out: the
number is not *missed*, it is gone from the query, so coverage stays pinned at 100 %. Only
`unit_conversion` — which rewrites a value — appears as lost coverage (79.14 %, barely half its
queries keeping all numbers). H5 must be stated over coverage **and** the query-vs-gold number count.

**5. S2's by-dose strata were misattributed, not merely uncertain.** Of the 730 dev queries S2 called
dose 5, **193** show five modifications in the TEXTO. The corrected table also exposes a confound the
old one hid: concepts per cell fall 27 → 17 → 8 → 2 → 1 as dose rises, so a slope read off these rows
confounds dose with family — which is why H4 is answered on E3 in S8 and not here.

## What is now known to be wrong

1. **"The previous study's operating point may not transfer."** The design treated this as a live
   threat. It is false: 0.60/0.35 is the argmax in 4 of 6 cells and the exceptions are noise (D-036).
2. **"`bge_m3_sparse` may survive L1 variation because learned sparse expands tokens."** My own
   framing in D-012, and wrong. Its identity ceiling is **0.0501** scored (0.0509 over all 35,422):
   it cannot retrieve a document from
   that document's own text. Its vectors are not L2-normalised (norms 0.47–1.09) against a raw dot
   product, so a sibling with a heavier vector out-scores the document against its own. A ceiling, not
   a defect — and the arm has no headroom left to lose.
3. **"The identity shortfall of `bge_m3_colbert` was a method property."** 484 of its 492 misses were
   undecidable duplicates; 8 remain of 34,646. G1 called this arm *ambiguous* on the strength of a
   corpus defect. **G1 is not re-read** (D-032), but the reading is now known to have been about the
   corpus.
4. **"The harness runs the method set."** It ran the five arms S2 needed. Widening to ten broke it
   twice: five retrievers scored every query in one block and lost **12 of 17 runs** to
   `DeadKernelError` (H6), and `bge_m3_dense`'s builder wrote its FAISS index outside
   `index/{collection}/` so both its runs failed *after* the build reported success, 70,242 docs and a
   clean stamp (H7). `num_docs` is not an integrity check.
5. **"A clean tree stamps clean."** A host/container line-ending disagreement stamped all seven
   indexes `code_dirty: true` over a file nobody had touched (H5). The same phantom explains the two
   dirty paths on the S1 `resumen` run archived under A2 — that archiving was right, but for a
   different reason than stated.
6. **"`requirements.txt`'s pin runs the method set."** `Alibaba-NLP/gte-multilingual-base` cannot be
   loaded under it by either path (D-034).
7. **"The manuscript's GTE rows identify their model."** Config, prose and results table name three
   different things; those rows are not reproducible from `(config, code commit)` (D-035). The third
   reporting defect of that submission, after the unreported `hiiamsid` row and the wrong README table.
8. **"`index/*/meta.json` stamps what produced an index."** It records `{python, sklearn}` and not the
   ML stack, so for a neural arm operating rule 1's provenance set is incomplete (H4).
